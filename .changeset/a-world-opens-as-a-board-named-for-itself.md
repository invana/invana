A world and a guardrail open as boards, named for themselves (WO15 · GR14 · WO16).

Drilling into a world in Govern now opens `world:<lens_id>` in `mainSection`, and the tab carries
the world's own name — `EU · H1 2026`, never `World`. A guardrail opens the same page under
`guardrail:<lens_id>`. The design has always drawn a lens this way; Studio had the drawer and no
board, so the drill-in stopped at a 420px column.

**The board is not the drawer widened.** The drawer stays the *picking* reading — the kit's layer
sections, with the run that prompted the narrowing still open beside it. The board is the *auditing*
one: what the lens narrows, how it has been used, every rule as an addressable row, the cast
resolved against the effective guardrails, and `spec.json`. Only the second can be kept — a declared
board's `Save report` freezes the resolved document, and *what was this world when the run happened*
is the question a live drawer cannot answer an hour later.

One composer draws both kinds, because a world and a guardrail are one `lenses` row separated by
`kind`. `kind` changes what the page says and nothing about how it is built: the crumb, and a usage
band that reads *in force on every run* rather than counting the runs that picked it.

The board reads. `Edit` puts the Govern panel back on that lens, drilled in, in one write; `Promote`,
`Duplicate` and `Delete` stay on the drill-in, where *what stops* and *what stays* can be read at the
moment of the act. A guardrail's `Edit` is absent without the permission, never greyed.

The two kinds join the board registry in the engine and in Studio, so `?page=world:<id>` survives a
reload and a shared link, and `More` on the drill-in header finds the board again after its tab is
closed.
