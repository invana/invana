A refusal is drawn where the click was, and a board with no body says which it is.

**Binding from the agent's panel swallowed its refusal.** The picker there binds as you click —
which is the whole argument for not staging binds into `Save`: a refusal that arrived alongside six
other edits could not say which one it was about. But the engine's `409` had nowhere to land, so a
refused bind looked like a chip that simply did not light. Both sides now draw the **same card from
the same payload** — under the agent's row on the Bindings tab, under the skill's chip on the
agent's picker — and Studio composes no sentence of its own for it.

**A lens refusal draws the plan's declared layers, the denied one struck.** Naming only the shut
band said what was closed and not what the skill needed, so a plan reaching four bands and refused
on one read like a plan that only ever wanted that band. The refusal carries `layers`, in the
plan's own order. An envelope refusal carries no strip: it is about a `step_key` and read no bands.

**A standing names the agent's world.** A world changes *which model decides*, so the agent row
says which one rather than asking the reader to hold the mapping. It is context and nothing else —
the bind check reads guardrails and never worlds, so a world is drawn beside a refusal and never
inside one.

**A bound draft is bound, and offered to nothing.** A skill has no deactivation; the thing that
keeps one out of a run is not publishing it. The chip says so and stays clickable, because binding
a draft is how a binding is prepared before the text is ready. Beside it, the picker states what the
bindings cost — in characters, never in tokens.

**A declared board kind with no body refuses by name.** `plan_runs` was the last kind falling
through the page host's dashboard branch, so it drew a step dashboard and read as a defect in that
composer rather than as a board nobody has written yet. The branch is exhaustive now, and the two
bodies that read a trace **check** the optional `runId` instead of casting it.
